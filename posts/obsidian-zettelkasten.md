## TL;DR

- **Obsidian** = a free notes app that uses plain markdown files.
- **Zettelkasten** = a note-taking method built around small, atomic, linked notes.
- Together = a "second brain" that's searchable forever.
- **5-minute install**, lifetime payoff.

## What is Obsidian?

A markdown-based notes app. Your notes are just `.md` files in a folder on your computer. Obsidian gives you a nice editor, links between notes, a graph view of your connections, and a ton of plugins.

Reasons it's worth it:

- **Free** for personal use.
- Works **offline**.
- Notes are **plain markdown** files. No cloud lock-in. You own them forever.
- **Linked notes** are first-class. Type `[[` and Obsidian autocompletes other notes.

## What is Zettelkasten?

A note-taking method invented by a German sociologist named Niklas Luhmann. He used index cards. The rules:

- Each note is **small** (one idea, paragraph-ish).
- Each note **links** to other notes.
- Notes are **atomic**: one note = one thing.

Over time, your notes form a network. When you go looking for "X", you find X plus everything connected to it.

> [!note] Why "atomic" matters
> If a note covers 5 topics, you can only link TO that note as a whole. If each topic is its own note, you can link to each one independently. The network gets way denser.

## How do I get started?

1. Download Obsidian: [obsidian.md](https://obsidian.md/).
2. Pick a folder for your vault. Anywhere on your computer.
3. Make **one** note. Write down something you learned today.
4. Tomorrow, make another. If it relates to the first, link to it.

That's it.

> [!tip] Don't overthink the system
> Plenty of people get paralyzed building elaborate templates and folder structures. Just write. The system **emerges from the notes you actually take**, not from planning ahead.
